The real performance ratio: actual weather, not a fantasy clear sky

Every day's clear-sky ceiling is now scaled by that day's own measured clearness index (actual irradiance ÷ clear-sky irradiance, from NASA's satellite data) before comparing it to your array's output. This is the step that finally separates "it was cloudy" from "something's wrong."

This is the payoff of the whole exercise. The weather-adjusted performance ratio lands at 74.3% — a textbook-normal number for a real system (typical residential arrays run 75–85%), not the physically-suspicious 98% or overly pessimistic numbers from earlier, guess-based versions. That's strong evidence this model is now properly calibrated.

The 18 days that underperformed for their actual weather

Notice the pattern: several of these land the day after a very cloudy/snowy day (Dec 3 → Dec 4, Mar 6 → Mar 7) — the storm clears, the sky is bright again, but output stays low. That's the signature of snow sitting on the panels rather than the storm itself blocking the sun.
